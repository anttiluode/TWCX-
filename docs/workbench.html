What this does not show

    
    
      
  • Seven coin cells of one chemistry from one lab. That is enough to see a large effect, not enough to qualify a plan for a production line. Run the planner on your own cells before relying on it.
    • 
      
  • The task is tracking fade against each cell's own commissioning reference. Cold grading of an unknown cell was also checked: absolute capacity came out about 3 mAh wrong on ~35 mAh cells whatever was measured, close to guessing the average.
    • 
      
  • The frequencies chosen are not new. The original authors' own code singles out the imaginary part at 17.8 Hz as the top feature. Simple existing selection rules find the same band: under the same protocol, ranking by correlation or a LASSO path scored 3.50 pp at 0.5 s and beat this planner on 5 of 7 cells, and ARD ranking tied it at 2 s (2.64 pp). A single 17.8 Hz reading from the literature scored 3.03 pp with no selection at all. Giving the full-sweep model one lengthscale per feature improves it from 4.14 to 3.52 pp; the 2 s plan still beats it on 6 of 7 cells. What this page adds is the cost model, the held-out comparison against the full sweep and 1 kHz, and a tool you can run on your own cells.
    • 
      
  • Frequency labels are a reconstructed 60-point log grid from 20 kHz to 0.02 Hz. Test times come from a stated model of three periods plus 0.2 s per frequency, not from an instrument.
    • 
      
  • A bigger budget did not help. At 60 s one fold picked a 0.05 Hz point that fit the training cells and failed on the held-out one. With six training cells, selection is noisy; the band is stable, the exact points are not.
    • 
      
  • The hindsight row shows what a perfect choice of one frequency would have scored. The planner leaves about 0.6 pp on the table at the 2 s budget.
